# OGAP - Methods (mathematical specification)

**Status:** methods draft for the NMI submission. Every formulation below is (i)
grounded in a specific primary source with its equation, and (ii) matched to the
implementation that computes it (file references in `ogap/`). Design choices that
depart from a source, and the assumptions each estimator makes, are stated
explicitly - a claim that is not supported by both the cited equation and the
code is flagged rather than asserted.

Notation. A case is a multi-channel volume `x ∈ R^{M×D×H×W}` (`M` MR contrasts,
canonical order T1n, T1ce, T2w, T2-FLAIR) with voxel labels
`y ∈ {0,1,2,3}^{D×H×W}` in the BraTS convention (0 background, 1 necrotic core,
2 oedema, 3 enhancing tumour). The evaluation regions are whole tumour
`WT = {1,2,3}`, tumour core `TC = {1,3}`, enhancing tumour `ET = {3}`. The
acquisition field strength `B₀ ∈ [0.064, 7] T` is treated as an explicit domain
variable; the deployment target is ultra-low-field (0.064 T portable, Hyperfine
Swoop). `Tφ` denotes the high-capacity teacher and `Sθ` the INT8-deployable
student.

---

## 1. Physics-grounded low-field domain randomisation

OGAP trains predominantly on high-field data (BraTS-glioma, UTSW, Erasmus; mostly
1.5-3 T) but must deploy at 0.064 T. We therefore **forward-degrade** high-field
volumes through an explicit MR acquisition model - a *physically constrained*
domain randomisation. This direction (high→low) is information-destroying and
therefore safe; the converse (low→high GAN synthesis) cannot recover pathology
the low-field acquisition never captured (Lucas et al., *Radiology* 2025, LowGAN:
white-matter-lesion Dice ceiling 0.32 despite SSIM 0.88; Campbell-Washburn et
al., *MRM* 2023: "image quality ≠ information content"). Implementation:
`MRIPhysicsAugmentor` (CPU, per-case) and `GPUPhysicsAugmentor` (batched,
on-device) in `ogap/legacy.py`.

### 1.1 Forward signal model

Each contrast is treated as a steady-state spin-echo (SE) or inversion-recovery
(IR/FLAIR) acquisition. With proton density `ρ` and sequence timings `(TR, TE,
TI)`, the Bloch steady-state signal is

```
S_SE(B₀)  = ρ · (1 - e^{-TR/T1(B₀)}) · e^{-TE/T2(B₀)}                       (1)
S_IR(B₀)  = ρ · |1 - 2 e^{-TI/T1(B₀)} + e^{-TR/T1(B₀)}| · e^{-TE/T2(B₀)}     (2)
```

(Haacke et al. 1999, Ch. 4; Lu et al., *JMRI* 2005, Eqs. 1-2 - the same closed
forms OGAP uses.) The pulse-sequence proxies are T1w `(TR,TE)=(600,10) ms`, T2w
`(4500,90) ms`, FLAIR `(TR,TE,TI)=(9000,110,2400) ms`
(`MRIPhysicsAugmentor._signal_se/_signal_ir/_modality_signal_scalar`).

### 1.2 Field-strength relaxometry and the T1↓/T2↑ asymmetry (load-bearing)

`T1(B₀)` and `T2(B₀)` are tabulated per tissue class {WM, GM, CSF, oedema,
enhancing} and interpolated **linearly in log B₀** between measured anchors
(`_T1_TABLE_MS`, `_T2_TABLE_MS`, `_interp_log_b0`). The anchors are taken from
*measured* values, not a single extrapolated power law:

- 0.064 T: O'Reilly & Webb, *MRM* 2022 (50 mT) and Jordanova et al., *MAGMA*
  2023 (0.064 T): WM T1 ≈ 254-275 ms, GM T1 ≈ 327-377 ms, WM/GM T2 ≈ 80-105 ms.
- 1.5/3 T: Wansapura et al., *JMRI* 1999 (in-vivo: WM 832 / GM 1331 ms T1 at 3 T,
  the exact LUT values); Stanisz et al., *MRM* 2005 (in-vitro reference).

The **mathematically essential point** is that `T1` and `T2` move in **opposite**
directions with field. `T1` shortens markedly toward low field - driven by the
macromolecular pool whose exchange rate follows an inverse-linear law
`Rm = 12.2·B₀^{-1.00} s⁻¹` (Wang et al., *NeuroImage* 2020, two-pool MT fit,
R²=0.997) - while `T2` is *weakly* field-dependent and slightly *lengthens* at
low field (Liu/Wu et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2021, 0.055 T: GM/WM T2 ≈ 110/100 ms vs
3 T 110/80 ms). The Bloembergen-Purcell-Pound / Solomon spectral-density theory
explains both: with `J(ω) = τc/(1+ω²τc²)`,

```
1/T1 ∝ J(ω₀) + 4 J(2ω₀)          (field-dependent; minimum at ω₀τc ≈ 1)
1/T2 ∝ 3 J(0) + 5 J(ω₀) + 2 J(2ω₀)   (dominated by the field-independent J(0))
```

so a single monotone power law applied to *both* relaxation times is physically
wrong. OGAP's table reproduces the asymmetry directly (e.g. WM: T1 830→230 ms but
T2 70→90 ms from 3 T→0.064 T), and the contrast warp consequently *reduces* GM-WM
T1 separability at low field exactly as observed in vivo (Liu/Wu).

### 1.3 Contrast warp

For target `B₀` and assumed source `B₀,src` (= 3 T, the training modal field), each
foreground channel is partitioned into low/mid/high intensity terciles, each
tercile mapped to a tissue class per contrast, and rescaled by the signal ratio

```
r = clip( S(B₀; tissue) / S(B₀,src; tissue), 0.1, 4.0 )                      (3)
```

(`_simulate_field_strength_contrast`; GPU port
`GPUPhysicsAugmentor._simulate_field_strength_contrast_batch`, which warps to the
**same** per-sample `B₀` already sampled for the noise/resolution operators, so
contrast, SNR and resolution are mutually coherent). Enhancing tumour on the
contrast channel uses a Gd-shortened T1 curve (§1.4). This is the single most
impactful low-field lever; it is enabled in the teacher run at per-sample
probability 0.5.

### 1.4 Gadolinium enhancement (Solomon-Bloembergen-Morgan)

Contrast-agent T1-shortening is the fast-exchange paramagnetic relaxation
enhancement limit,

```
1/T1,post = 1/T1,pre + r1 · [Gd]                                             (4)
```

(Solomon, *Phys. Rev.* 1955; Caravan et al., *Chem. Rev.* 1999). The relaxivity
`r1 ≈ 4-5 mM⁻¹s⁻¹` for Gd-DTPA is itself mildly field-dependent (Rooney 2007:
~5.2 at low field → ~3.6 at 7 T); OGAP encodes the net effect through the
enhancing-tumour T1 curve, which shortens faster than WM as `B₀` falls, so the
ET/WM contrast ratio at 0.064 T is ≈3× that at 3 T.

### 1.5 Rician noise (object-based estimation + complex-domain injection)

Low-field SNR is coil/Johnson-noise-limited and ∝ B₀ (Pohmann et al., *MRM*
2016). Noise is **injected** exactly, in the complex domain, then magnitude-taken
(Coupé et al., *Med. Image Anal.* 2010, Eq. 14):

```
m = sqrt( (A + η₁)² + η₂² ),   η₁, η₂ ~ N(0, σ²)                              (5)
```

which is exactly Rician and assumption-light. The noise level σ is *calibrated*
from data with the robust object-based estimator of Coupé et al. (the McGill/MNI
method OGAP's lineage shares): a first-level 3D Haar transform, object extraction
from the LLL subband (high-gradient voxels excluded), and the median-absolute-
deviation of the matching HHH coefficients,

```
σ̂ = median(|y_HHH|) / 0.6745                                                 (6)
```

followed by a **single-shot low-SNR correction** - a bounded approximation of the
Koay iterative Rician bias correction (the code does NOT run the full ξ(θ) Bessel
fixed-point):

```
σ̂_n = σ̂ · c(SNR),   c = 1 if SNR ≥ 3, else max( sqrt((2 + SNR²)/(4 + SNR²)), 0.5 )   (7)
```

with `SNR = object-mean / σ̂` (`_estimate_rician_sigma`, legacy.py:3861). The exact
Koay correction this approximates is `σ̂_n = sqrt(σ̂²/ξ(θ))` with
`ξ(θ) = 2 + θ² - (π/8) e^{-θ²/2}[(2+θ²)I₀(θ²/4) + θ²I₁(θ²/4)]²` iterated by
`θ_{t+1} = sqrt(ξ(θ_t)(1 + m̄/σ̂) - 2)` (Coupé et al. 2010, Eqs. 7-9; I₀,I₁ modified
Bessel). We use the bounded single-shot form because σ here only sets an
*augmentation* noise level, not a quantitative measurement - but the methods text
must not present the exact Bessel fixed-point as if it were implemented. The
target σ is scaled
to the sampled field by `σ_tgt = σ_src·(B₀,src/B₀,tgt)` (SNR ∝ B₀). **Stated
limitation:** Eqs. 6-8 assume single-coil, stationary Rician noise; under
multi-coil / parallel (GRAPPA/SENSE) reconstruction - common on budget LMIC
scanners - the noise is non-central χ and σ̂ is biased (Coupé et al., §future
work). The injection Eq. 5 is unaffected; only the *calibration* carries the
caveat.

### 1.6 Other acquisition operators

Each is gated by an independent Bernoulli probability and, where physical, by
`B₀`: (a) low-frequency **bias field** (smooth multiplicative polynomial /
B1 inhomogeneity); (b) single-channel **receive-coil falloff** (anisotropic
Gaussian, floor 0.55, gated to `B₀ ≤ 1 T` where SENSE/GRAPPA does not correct it;
Shetty et al., *RadioGraphics* 2023; Ljungberg et al., *HBM* 2025); (c)
**off-resonance susceptibility voids** near sinus/mastoid/brainstem (these *shrink*
at low field, so they are down-weighted vs high-field training data); (d) k-space
artefacts - Gibbs ringing, motion, ghosting, spike/herringbone (Koonjoo et al.,
*Sci. Rep.* 2021, inject in the sensor domain); (e) **anisotropic 2-D** thick-slice
acquisition (the dominant low-field geometry).

### 1.7 Auxiliary Fourier-basis augmentation (AFA)

Beyond physics, a data-agnostic frequency-domain perturbation fills the
augmentation gap (Vaish et al., MIDL 2025; Vaish, Wang & Strisciuglio 2024). Per
channel, a single planar wave is added:

```
x'_c = x_c + σ_c · A_{f,ω},   A_{f,ω}(u) = R·sin(2π f ⟨u, d(ω)⟩ - π/4)        (9)
```

with frequency `f ~ U[1, M_size]`, orientation `ω ~ U[0,π]`, and exponential
strength `σ_c ~ Exp(λ)` (mean 10-15 in the source). **Critical normalisation
(verified):** `R` makes the *wave* unit-norm - OGAP uses **unit-peak (max-abs)**
scaling. A unit-ℓ₂ norm taken over the whole 128³ volume would divide the per-voxel
amplitude by ≈√(128³) ≈ 1448, making the perturbation ~10³× too small (a silent
no-op); unit-peak keeps the amplitude controlled by `σ_c` and the channel range
(`_fourier_amplitude_mix`).

### 1.8 Field-strength prior

The per-sample target `B₀` is drawn from a categorical prior over
{0.3, 0.55, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5, 3.0} T. The LMIC-weighted prior (`P(1.5 T)=0.52`,
`P(3 T)=0.16`) over-samples sub-3 T so ≈84% of training samples are degraded
toward low field - the realistic African deployment regime (Anazodo et al.,
*NMR Biomed.* 2023, CAMERA: 0.8 scanners/million; 39% obsolete sub-1 T still
clinical).

## 2. Knowledge distillation with the Hölder pseudo-divergence

The deployed student `Sθ` (INT8, CPU) is distilled from `Tφ`. The distillation
term is a temperature-scaled **Hölder projective pseudo-divergence** (HPD;
Nielsen, Sun & Marchand-Maillet, *Entropy* 2017, Def. 1) between teacher and
student class posteriors (`holder_divergence`, `ogap/legacy.py`):

```
D_α(t : s) = - log [ ⟨t, s⟩ / ( ‖t‖_α · ‖s‖_β ) ],   1/α + 1/β = 1            (10)
L_HPD = T² · D_α( softmax(z_T/T), softmax(z_S/T) )
```

with conjugate exponents `β = α/(α-1)`. Hölder's inequality gives the ratio ≤ 1,
hence `L_HPD ≥ 0`; `α = 2 (⇒ β = 2)` is the Cauchy-Schwarz divergence, the unique
**proper** member (`D₂(p:p)=0`). The `T²` factor is the Hinton KD gradient scaling.

**Semantics of `α ≠ 2` (deliberate; pre-registered ablation).** HPD is *improper*
for `α ≠ 2`: minimising `D_α(t:s)` over the student does **not** drive `s → t`.
The equality condition `t^α ∝ s^β` gives the student optimum

```
s* ∝ t^{α-1}   (renormalised).                                              (11)
```

We verified this numerically against the implementation: with the default
`α = 1.5`, the loss is minimised (→ 0) at `s = teacher^{0.5}` (renormalised) - a
**flatter, higher-entropy target** than the teacher - while `D(student=teacher) =
0.042 > 0`; at `α = 2`, `D(student=teacher) = 0`. We adopt `α = 1.5` as deliberate
**uncertainty transfer**: it preserves more of the teacher's secondary-class mass
(a learned, data-dependent label smoothing), which is appropriate for the
low-field/LMIC regime where calibrated boundary uncertainty matters more than
sharp arg-max matching (cf. the Hölder-uncertainty framing of Zhang et al.,
*IEEE TMM* 2024). The control `α = 2.0` (proper, exact teacher-matching) is run as
a paired ablation (`cmd_kd_compare` `holder_default`/`holder_proper`;
`cmd_holder_sweep` over `{1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 2.5}`). For `α ≤ 1` the
HPD limit degenerates toward a uniform target, so the implementation falls back to
standard KL (the correct floor), not the `α→1` HPD.

**Region weighting and confidence gating.** The KD term is summed over
two-channel (background-vs-region) log-sum-exp projections of WT/TC/ET with
weights `(1, 1.5, 3)` (the small ET region benefits most from teacher guidance),
and voxelwise-gated by teacher confidence `w = clip(max_c t_c / τ, 0, 1)` so the
student does not imitate unreliable teacher predictions. The full objective is

```
L = λ_task·L_task + λ_kd·L_HPD + λ_feat·L_feat + λ_attn·L_attn               (12)
```

with `L_task` a Dice+CE region loss; `L_feat` an (optional) normalised feature
MSE; and an opt-in Region-Aware-Attention term (PAT; Lin et al. 2025) for
heterogeneous teacher→student feature transfer.

## 3. Architectures

### 3.1 Teacher family

Teachers are interchangeable KD sources (never deployed): (a) a MedNeXt-style
3-D U-Net (`UNet3DTeacher`); (b) **SegMamba** (`SegMambaTeacher`, Xing et al.,
MICCAI 2024) - a stem plus tri-oriented spatial Mamba (TSMamba) blocks, each a
gated spatial convolution `GSC(z)=z+C₃(C₃(z)⊙C₁(z))`, a tri-oriented selective
scan `ToM(z)=Mamba(z_fwd)+Mamba(z_rev)+Mamba(z_slice)`, and an MLP, with
feature-uncertainty (FUE) skip filtering `z̃=z+z·(1-u)`, `u=-z̄ log z̄`,
`z̄=σ(mean_C z)`; (c) Swin UNETR (Hatamizadeh et al. 2022); (d) a continuous-depth
ODE teacher (§3.2). SegMamba's selective-scan kernel resists INT8 export, so it is
*teacher-only*; a pure-PyTorch fallback (`_MinimalMamba`) makes it CPU-testable
and `torch.compile`-able without the CUDA kernel.

Cross-scanner invariance is injected at the **teacher** via MixStyle/DSU feature
domain-randomisation after encoder blocks 1-2 (Zhou et al. 2021; Li et al. 2022),
so the KD logits themselves carry domain-invariant structure rather than only the
augmentation noise distribution. (Group-DRO worst-group training - Sagawa et al.,
ICLR 2020, `min_θ max_g E_{P_g}[ℓ] + C/√n_g` with online exponentiated-gradient
group weights - is a principled alternative we did *not* adopt: it requires hard
group labels and strong ℓ₂/early-stopping or it collapses to ERM on
over-parameterised nets; our robustness instead comes from §1 augmentation + the
§1.8 sampler + teacher feature-DR.)

### 3.2 Continuous-depth student and the memory caveat

The deployable student admits a weight-tied Neural-ODE bottleneck
`dh/dt = f_θ(h, t)` (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2018) integrated by a **fixed-step,
weight-tied K-step Euler unroll** `h_{k+1} = h_k + (1/K) f_θ(h_k)` - a single
weight set, hence INT8-quantisable. **We do not claim the Neural-ODE O(1)-memory
benefit for this path.** That benefit is a property of the *adjoint* method only;
back-propagating through a fixed-step solver costs O(K) memory like a K-block
ResNet. `ogap/numerics/odeint.py` enforces this honestly: fixed-step
Euler/midpoint/RK4 by default, and an adjoint request *raises* unless
`torchdiffeq` is installed, so a fixed-step ablation can never be mistaken for the
paper method. The claimed advantage of the unroll is therefore **parameter
efficiency / weight-tying**, not memory.

### 3.3 Deployed artifact

The student is exported to INT8 via ONNX + ONNX Runtime / OpenVINO (the torch
dynamic-quant proxy is strict-by-default and refuses conv models, so it can never
silently supply an "INT8" number). INT8 is the *only* reported deployment format.

## 4. Hardware-aware neural architecture search

The student backbone is selected by a constrained multi-objective search
(`ogap/nas/`), following the survey framework of White et al. 2023:

```
maximise  A_proxy(a)    subject to   size_INT8(a) ≤ B_size,   latency₁(a) ≤ B_lat
```

over architectures `a` (base width, block style, bottleneck depth, deep
supervision, feature-DR), returning a **Pareto front** over (accuracy↑, INT8 MB↓,
single-volume latency↓) - "one model per clinic", not a single winner. Search by
random or aging/regularised evolution (Real et al. 2019).

**Accuracy proxy.** The default is `jacob_cov` (NASWOT; Mellor et al. 2021): the
score `-Σ_i (log e_i + 1/e_i)` over the eigenvalues `e_i` of the `B×B` correlation
matrix of per-sample input-output Jacobians; it is maximal for a perfectly
decorrelated (expressive) network. **This is defined over a batch** `B`: a 1×1
correlation matrix is the constant `[[1]]`, so the proxy must be evaluated with
`B ≥ 16` (`proxy_batch`, decoupled from the `B=1` single-volume *cost* batch).
**Cost.** `latency₁` and peak activation are measured on a single volume (`B=1`,
the LMIC-edge deployment unit), independent of the proxy batch.

**Proxy validation (required before trusting the search).** Zero-cost proxies do
*not* reliably outperform #params/FLOPs baselines (White et al. 2023, §5). Before
a proxy drives the search, `ogap/nas/validation.py` computes its
Spearman/Kendall rank correlation against real partially-trained validation Dice
across a sample of architectures, with a permutation p-value and an explicit
trustworthy / anti-correlated verdict; the #params and FLOPs baselines are
reported alongside as the honest comparators.

## 5. Evaluation and statistical inference

### 5.1 Segmentation metrics

Per region `R ∈ {WT, TC, ET}`, Dice `= 2|P∩G| / (|P|+|G|)` and the 95th-percentile
Hausdorff distance

```
HD95(P,G) = max( d̃₉₅(P→G), d̃₉₅(G→P) )                                       (13)
```

(the max of directional 95th percentiles - concatenating then taking one
percentile underestimates). **Empty-region convention (must be reported):** if
exactly one of `P,G` is empty, `HD95 = 373.1287 mm` (the diagonal of a 240³ @1 mm
volume); if both are empty, `HD95 = 0`. This penalty dominates the ET mean (ET is
small and frequently absent), so reported HD95 is given **lesion-wise**
(BraTS-2023, per connected component, with the same penalty) and **after
connected-component post-processing** (largest-component retention per class, with
a size/confidence gate for legitimately small ET) - far-away false-positive
islands otherwise inflate raw HD95. Per-epoch training-log HD95 is *raw* and used
only as a training diagnostic, never quoted (`brats_metrics` docstring;
`evaluate --lesion_wise`; `ogap/legacy.py`).

### 5.2 Calibrated uncertainty: split-conformal coverage

For a per-case nonconformity score, split-conformal prediction sets the threshold
at the `⌈(n+1)(1-α)⌉`-th order statistic of the `n` calibration scores, giving
finite-sample marginal coverage `≥ 1-α`. For the *small per-group* calibration
sets typical of an LMIC cohort, the marginal mean is misleading; the
**per-realisation** coverage of a single predictor is Beta-distributed
(Sánchez-Domínguez et al. 2025):

```
C ~ Beta( k, n - k + 1 ),   k = ⌈(n+1)(1-α)⌉                                 (14)
```

(`ogap/ood/conformal.py::coverage_beta_params`). We report **group-balanced**
worst-group coverage - one conformal predictor per acquisition subgroup
(`GroupBalancedConformalPredictor`) - with Eq. 14 as the coverage law and a
Clopper-Pearson interval on the measured per-group coverage (the worst group is by
construction the smallest/noisiest). A continuous-normalising-flow density model
(`ogap/ood/cnf.py`) supplies an auxiliary out-of-distribution score.

### 5.3 Equity: per-subgroup risk disparity

The clinically actionable axis is the acquisition domain (field strength × site).
Following "Fairness Without Harm" (Pang et al., NeurIPS 2024, Def. 3.1) we report
the **signed risk disparity**

```
Δ_k = R_{Q_k} - R_Q                                                          (15)
```

(`ogap/evaluation/equity.py::risk_disparity`; `R` = expected loss, oriented so
`Δ_k > 0` means "subgroup k is served worse than the cohort"), with a bootstrap CI
and a Mann-Whitney gap test vs a reference group (e.g. 3 T). **We report `Δ_k`
signed, not `|Δ_k|` or `max_k`:** the theorem bounds the signed quantity, and its
lower-bound relation to demographic-parity / equalised-odds disparities holds only
under *balanced* subgroups (Pang et al., Prop. 3.1) - likely violated for scarce
low-field data - so we do not invoke the DP/EOd interpretation without
re-balancing.

### 5.4 Paired comparisons and INT8 non-inferiority

Two-method differences use the paired **Wilcoxon signed-rank** test with
**Holm** FWER control (BH-FDR optional), a rank-biserial effect size, and a
**BCa bootstrap** CI for the median paired difference (`ogap/evaluation/stats.py`).
Compression equivalence is established by **non-inferiority**, not a non-significant
two-sided test: with pre-registered margins `δ_Dice = 0.01` (below inter-rater
noise) and `δ_HD95 = 2 mm` (≤ 2× nominal voxel), INT8 is declared non-inferior to
FP32 for a metric iff the upper BCa CI bound of the median loss is below the
margin (`noninferiority_table`). The 15-way modality-robustness suite uses a
per-combination Friedman omnibus across the three models followed by Holm-corrected
pairwise Wilcoxon; the confirmatory family (full + single-modality combinations) is
globally Holm-controlled, the 2-/3-modality combinations are labelled exploratory.

## 6. Summary of assumptions and limitations (for the Methods/Limitations text)

1. The forward model (§1.1-1.4) assumes SE/IR steady state and tissue-classwise
   relaxometry; it does not model intra-class heterogeneity or true sequence
   physics (eddy currents, slice profile). It is a *domain-randomisation prior*,
   not a simulator.
2. Tumour/oedema `T1(B₀)` is the least-constrained part of the LUT (no multi-field
   tumour relaxometry exists); the WM macromolecular `B₀` law (Wang 2020) does not
   transfer to high-free-water tumour, so tumour curves are treated as approximate
   and the contrast warp is clipped (Eq. 3).
3. Rician σ estimation (Eqs. 6-8) assumes single-coil stationary noise; multi-coil
   parallel reconstruction breaks it (the injection Eq. 5 does not depend on it).
4. The Neural-ODE path claims parameter efficiency, **not** O(1) memory (§3.2).
5. Conformal/equity guarantees are finite-sample and per-subgroup; the worst group
   is the smallest and its interval the widest (§5.2-5.3) - reported as such.
6. There is essentially no real African 0.064 T training/validation data
   (Anazodo 2023); in-silico low-field validation has no in-distribution
   real-world check, which is the motivation for the prospective reader study and
   on-site DECIDE-AI feasibility arm (see the companion analysis-plan).
